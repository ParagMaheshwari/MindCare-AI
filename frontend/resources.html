Educational Wellness Guides

Practical, evidence-grounded coping strategies curated for academic, physical, and digital wellness.

Focus 3 min read

Stress Management

Identify triggers early. Use the "Brain Dump" technique to get swirling worries onto paper, and break large assignments into 25-minute single-focus blocks.

💡 Technique: 25-min single-focus study sprints
Sleep 2 min read

Sleep Hygiene

Maintain consistent sleep and wake times, keep your bedroom cool and dark, and put phones away at least 30 minutes before sleep to allow melatonin release.

🌙 Routine: 30-min pre-sleep screen blackout
Study 3 min read

Study-Life Balance

Adopt the Pomodoro technique (50 min focus / 10 min break). Remember that rest is not a reward you earn; it is a biological requirement for memory retention.

⏱️ Method: 50 min deep work / 10 min break
Digital 2 min read

Digital Wellness

Social media feeds are engineered for intermittent dopamine spikes. Set daily limits for feeds, turn off badge notifications, and create phone-free meal times.

📵 Boundary: Silent badge alerts & app limits
Grounding Quick reset

Relaxation & Grounding

Use the 5-4-3-2-1 sensory technique when feeling anxious: notice 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, and 1 you can taste.

👁️ Reset: 5-4-3-2-1 sensory countdown
Movement Daily habit

Physical Movement

Just 20 minutes of daily physical movement—brisk walking, cycling, or yoga—releases endorphins and physically burns off excess cortisol and study stress.

🏃 Habit: 20 min daily aerobic movement
Social Support

Social Connection

Schedule regular phone calls or study dates with supportive friends or family. Sharing your worries out loud helps dismantle the feeling of isolation.

💬 Support: Weekly intentional friend check-ins
Campus Free access

Campus Counseling Centers

Most universities offer free or low-cost confidential counseling, peer support groups, and stress management workshops for students. Don't hesitate to book a session.

🎓 Access: Free confidential campus visits
These resources provide general educational information only. They are not medical advice and do not replace personalized care from licensed mental-health professionals.
